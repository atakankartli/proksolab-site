Why proximity sensing is harder than it looks

Adding "knows when something is near" to a product sounds simple. Then the real problems show up.

🔋

It drains the battery

A sensor that is always listening can cost more energy than the rest of the product. Battery life becomes the first complaint in reviews.

📄

Datasheets oversell

Best-case numbers measured in perfect conditions rarely survive a real enclosure, real materials and real users.

🧩

Integration takes months

Tuning, enclosure effects and false triggers eat engineering time that should go into your actual product.

Average = active mA × (ms × rate ÷ 1000) + sleep µA ÷ 1000. Life = capacity × usable share ÷ average. Ignores self-discharge, temperature and the rest of your circuit. Defaults are a coin cell and example values, not our specs.

Science first: every number has a label

Sensor specs are easy to inflate. Every figure we give you says where it came from, so you know which ones you can design around.

MEASURED

Taken on the bench or in your enclosure, with the raw data and test setup handed to you.

CALCULATED

Worked out from measured values, with the formula written next to it.

ESTIMATED

Engineering judgement, flagged clearly. We never quote one as a spec.

Questions

Can we test before buying in volume?

Yes. Start with an evaluation kit and our measurement data, and check the results on your own bench.

Will our enclosure change the performance?

Usually, yes. Plastics, glass and metal near the sensor all matter. That is why we measure inside your real enclosure, not just on an open bench.

Can you design a custom sensor?

Yes. Tell us your range, size, power budget and environment, and we will tell you what is realistic.

How do we connect it to our product?

Tell us which microcontroller and interface you use and we will confirm how the sensor connects.

Make your product aware, not power hungry.

Tell us what you are building and we will suggest a sensor or an evaluation kit. We reply within one working day.
